# DRIFT - visual revisit brainstorm

28 November 2026. Brainstorm only: nothing here is decided or built yet.
Based on the 0.1.0 build (main), the PRD, the project log, and a look at
every mode running in the preview pane.

Effort scale used below: **S** = a session or less, **M** = a few
sessions, **L** = a project in itself.

## 2. Is this the limit?

**No. We are close to the ceiling of one specific approach, not of the
platform.**

Everything visual today is generated by code (boxes, shader noise, pixel
functions) plus five small images. That approach has roughly peaked: more
code tends to give "more detailed boxes", not a different class of look.

What actually limits us, in order:

1. **Assets, not the engine.** Three.js in a browser can show authored
   3D models with proper materials and baked lightmaps at good quality.
   We have no authored models at all. This is the single biggest lever.
2. **The integrated-GPU budget.** The target is 60 fps on Intel UHD P630.
   This rules out live shadows, SSAO, volumetric fog, MSAA and heavy
   per-pixel shaders. It does **not** rule out good models, baked
   lighting, decent textures, particles or cheap screen effects: those
   cost little per frame.
3. **2D modes on Canvas 2D.** Tactical, cockpit and Vesper are drawn
   with the plain canvas API. It is fine for lines and sprites, weak for
   lighting, glow and lots of particles. Moving one of them to WebGL
   (Three.js orthographic camera, or a small 2D WebGL layer) raises its
   ceiling a lot.
4. **Licensing.** Any outside art must be CC0 or compatible and is
   already excluded from the MIT grant. AI-generated art is usable but
   should be recorded the same way.

**The real decision:** stay fully procedural (cheaper, consistent,
lower ceiling) or open an asset pipeline (GLTF models from CC0 packs,
AI 3D tools or Blender, plus baked lightmaps). Most of the "L" items
below assume the pipeline.

## 3. Visual improvements by mode

### 0. Bug found during this review
- **Bridge whites out during cruise.** In the preview pane, the bridge
  interior goes almost fully white for the whole trip; with bloom turned
  off it looks normal. Something on the bridge (probably the warp trails
  or a ceiling emitter) is far too bright, or invalid, for the bloom
  pass. Needs a check in a normal browser, then a fix. **S**

### 2. Ship exploration (interior)
Today: clean, readable, but generic boxes; every room reads the same.
- Room identity: signage, floor markings, decals, per-room colour and
  clutter (crates in cargo, glassware in the lab, cables in engineering). **S-M**
- Hero props as real models: cryo pods with frosted glass and faces,
  reactor core, helm chair. **M** (needs pipeline)
- Cheap atmosphere: dust motes in light beams, fake light shafts
  (camera-facing cards), condensation drips in cryo, steam vents. **S**
- Animated screens with real content instead of static images. **S**
- Lightmaps baked in Blender instead of per-vertex light: sharp shadows
  and bounce light at zero per-frame cost. **L** (replaces current bake)

### 3-4. Exterior view and travel (biggest gap)
Today: the hull is grey boxes with flat light; it is the weakest image
in the game and it is the "money shot".
- Proper hull: a modelled ship (or a much richer procedural one) with
  panel normal maps, greebles, antenna, radiator fins. **M-L**
- Lighting: hard sun key light, coloured rim light from the destination
  body, running lights and window glow. **S**
- Engines: glowing nozzles and a shader exhaust plume that follows
  speed. **S**
- Warp: a tunnel or stretch effect at departure and a flash on arrival,
  instead of only star streaks. **S-M**
- Camera: several shot types per trip (fly-by, chase, wide reveal of the
  destination) instead of one follow shot. **S-M**
- Destinations: arrival reveal shots; Vesper's broken ring with sparks
  and drifting debris. **M**

### 5. Command deck
Today: the most polished screen.
- Panel open/close animation, subtle flicker and scanline. **S**
- Hologram ship should match whatever the new hull becomes. **S** after hull.

### 7. Tactical combat (2D hologram)
Today: clean but sparse; hits and kills have little weight.
- Juice: glowing additive lines, explosion rings, debris, shield ripple
  on hit, missile smoke trails, hit-stop, small screen shake. **S**
- Background: layered holo grid with parallax, faint asteroid shadows. **S**
- The cockpit renderer already has particles and shake; reuse its ideas. 

### 8. Cockpit combat
Today: the strongest 2D mode; raiders fly on one level near the horizon.
- Bigger explosions, wreck pieces tumbling past the canopy, laser bolts
  with glow, shield impact on the glass, cracks as hull drops, console
  lights flickering when hit. **S-M**
- Rebuild the cockpit view in Three.js on the same 2D rules (real 3D
  raider meshes, small height bob). Big jump in depth. **M-L**
- A true 3D dogfight (raiders above and below) is a different game rule
  set. **L**, already out of scope in the PRD.

### 9. Vesper blackout (2D stealth)
Today: readable, but flat dark rooms and very few effects.
- Drone-camera feel: scanlines, grain, slight lens distortion, signal
  glitch when a guard is near. **S**
- Real 2D lighting: walls cast shadows from the lamp (the sight-cone
  polygon code already does this maths), flickering emergency lights,
  coloured light from sockets and the portal. **M**
- Effects: sparks from broken panels, freeze-beam and ice burst, portal
  swirl, alien sprites with animation, red alert screen pulse. **S-M**
- Moving the renderer to WebGL would make lighting and particles cheap. **M**

### 10. Companions and drone frames
- Distinct silhouettes per frame, not only colours. **S-M**
- Natural home for unlockable skins (see section 5).

### Cross-cutting
- **Three different UI languages today:** the deck and combat are cyan
  holo, Vesper is a green "drone operations" console with different type.
  Pick one design language with variants per context. **S-M**
- One shared colour and font file instead of colours spread through the
  code (roughly 500 hard-coded colours today across UI, combat, Vesper
  and world). Required before any skin system. **M**

## 4. Landing page

Today: the title is "DRIFT", one line of fiction and Begin, over the
ship interior, with a controls line at the bottom. It does not say what
you can do, that there are three kinds of play, that it is desktop-only,
or that it saves.

**Open question first:** the PRD says the audience is the author only,
but the game is now public on GitHub and Vercel. A landing page is for
other people, so the PRD's "users" line should change if we build one.

Options:

**A. Richer in-game title (stay in one page)**
- Attract mode: slow cinematic orbit of the ship behind the title.
- Main menu: Begin/Continue, How to play, Settings (volume, quality),
  About / GitHub.
- Three mode cards: Explore the ship, Defend it, Salvage Vesper, each
  with a still or short loop and one line.
- Controls sheet per mode, shown once and from the menu.
- Effort **S-M**.

**B. Separate landing page, game at `/play`**
- Hero image or short looping video, one-paragraph pitch, the three
  modes, screenshots, controls, "Play in your browser" button, GitHub
  link, honest status (prototype, desktop only, no account).
- Mobile visitors get a real page and a "play on desktop" note instead
  of a game that cannot be controlled.
- Good for sharing links (proper preview image and description).
- Effort **S-M**; static HTML, no new dependencies.

**Recommendation:** B for first-time and mobile visitors plus the
attract mode from A, which is cheap and makes the game itself feel
finished. The landing page should wait for the visual pass so its
screenshots show the new look.

## 5. Skins

"Skin" here means a whole-game look: palette, UI type, surface textures,
post-processing grade, and (for bigger skins) props and models.

### Candidate skins

| Skin | Look | Fits | Effort |
| ---- | ---- | ---- | ------ |
| **Cryo Teal** (current) | Clean panels, cyan holo, cool light | Baseline | - |
| **Cassette Futurism** | Beige and off-white panels, chunky switches, amber or green monochrome CRT screens, warm practical lights | The lonely, analogue mood of the PRD; strongest identity | L (new props and UI) |
| **Derelict** | Rust, grime, emergency red and sodium orange, heavy flicker | Vesper and a "ship in decline" story beat | M |
| **Hard Sci-Fi** | White and grey panels, stencil labels, gold foil on the hull, NASA-style realism | Exterior and travel | M |
| **Neon Grid** | Black, magenta and cyan, glowing grid lines | Tactical and cockpit combat | S-M |
| **Blueprint Holo** | Everything rendered as blue linework and glow | Cheap, very stylised, light on the GPU | M |
| **Pastel Low-Poly** | Flat soft colours, simple shapes, no textures | Calm vibe, fastest on weak GPUs | L |
| **Pixel 16-bit** | Pixel-art sprites and tiles for the 2D modes only | Vesper and tactical | M |

### How skins could be offered
1. **Settings option:** player picks a skin from the menu.
2. **Unlockables:** earned like the Vesper Scout (beat Ace, recover all
   cells). Gives the game a reward loop.
3. **One new art direction:** pick a single skin to become the game's
   look, no switching.
4. **Per-mode skins:** each mode keeps its own look (for example Derelict
   for Vesper, Neon Grid for combat).

### What a skin system needs (feasibility)
- A theme file with design tokens (colours, fonts, glow, grade) read by
  CSS, the canvas renderers and the Three.js materials. Today's colours
  are spread through the code, so this is the prerequisite. **M**
- Texture sets per skin. The `public/art/wall|floor|ceiling` drop folders
  already swap ship surfaces, so this seam half-exists.
- A colour grade per skin (tint, contrast, scanlines, grain) in the post
  pass. Cheap on the GPU. **S**
- Model variants for skins that change shapes (Cassette Futurism,
  Pastel). This needs the asset pipeline.

Palette-and-grade skins (Neon Grid, Derelict, Blueprint) are cheap once
the token file exists. Shape-changing skins are the expensive ones.

## 6. Suggested order (for discussion)

1. Fix the bridge whiteout. **S**
2. Theme token file and one UI language. **M** (unblocks skins, tidies UI)
3. Cheap high-impact visuals: exterior lighting and engines, warp
   effect, tactical combat juice, Vesper camera feel. **S each**
4. Decide on the asset pipeline. If yes: hull model and hero props.
5. First alternative skin (cheapest: Neon Grid or Derelict).
6. Landing page and attract mode, with screenshots of the new look.
